Accessibility

Built for everyone.

What we aim for, what we're still fixing, and how to tell us when something gets in your way.

Who this is for

This statement covers The Com'mon People website (the-common-people.com) and the Comquat tools that go with it:

  • CV Rewrite, the CV Builder and the pitch video, at cv.the-common-people.com
  • Interview Prep, at prep.the-common-people.com
  • Comquat for Recruiters, at recruiters.the-common-people.com

They're all run by Comquat Ventures UK Ltd. We want everyone to be able to use them, whatever device you're on and however you use it, including if you're disabled, neurodivergent or just having a tough day.

What you should be able to do

  • Zoom in, and the pages rearrange to fit so you don't have to scroll sideways.
  • Get around using just a keyboard. Every page starts with a Skip to main content link, and a clear outline shows where you are.
  • Turn on Easy read in the Comquat tools, for bigger, plainer text and a button that reads the page out loud.
  • Talk instead of typing in the CV Builder and Interview Prep.
  • Have less movement on screen: if your device is set to reduce motion, we switch our animations off.
  • Make a pitch video one small step at a time, with captions if you want them.

How accessible this is

We aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA. Right now we meet most of it, but not all of it yet, because of the things listed below.

What we know isn't right yet

  • The Commons cartoon episodes have their words in the picture, and each one that's out has a written version underneath with every word and what's happening on screen. The words in the picture can't be resized or turned off, and there's no spoken description of the pictures yet.
  • A robot copy of the NVDA screen reader reads seven of our main pages every month, and they all pass. We haven't yet tested every page by hand with a screen reader such as JAWS or VoiceOver, or with disabled people using the sites for real.
  • We haven't yet checked every CV design and report you can download (Word and PDF) with assistive technology. The Word and ATS designs are the plainest.
  • Styling a pitch video, and blurring or changing its background, need an up-to-date browser such as Chrome, Edge or Safari. In an older browser you can still record a plain video.

What we've fixed

After our review on 21 November 2026 we:

  • made small orange and teal text darker on light backgrounds, so it's easier to read
  • added a Skip to main content link and a main area to every page
  • gave picture links and buttons names that screen readers can announce
  • made small labels at least 13 pixels, and footer links bigger to tap (we're working towards 14 pixels for all readable text)
  • set every page to UK English, so screen readers pronounce words properly
  • put every heading in order and gave every part of each page a clear area, so screen reader users can jump around easily
  • split the pitch video into five short steps, one on screen at a time, with tips and options tucked away until you want them
  • added a written version of each Commons episode, with every word and what's happening on screen, under the video

How we check

On 21 November 2026 we checked every page of all four sites, on laptop and phone sizes, with an automated checker (axe-core) against WCAG 2.2 AA. Then we checked by hand: getting around with just a keyboard, seeing where you are on the page, colour contrast, phone-sized screens and reduced motion.

Since 21 November 2026, a real copy of NVDA (the free Windows screen reader) also reads seven of our main pages out loud every month. It checks that the Skip to main content link comes first, that each page has one main heading and sensible headings under it, and that every link and button it reaches has a name.

Both checks run every month, and we update this statement when it finds something new.

Tell us if something doesn't work for you

If you find something hard to use, or you need something in a different format such as plain text or large print, email neil.copping@the-common-people.com. Tell us the page and what got in your way, and we'll fix it or find another way to help. If it's easier, ask someone you trust to email for you.
