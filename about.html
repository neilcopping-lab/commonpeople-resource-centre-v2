01 · The story

About
The Com'mon People.

We're a free resource centre for the people on both sides of recruitment. We give away everything we know because we think the whole process works better when both sides understand each other.

The Com'mon People started from a fairly simple observation. The recruitment industry produces a lot of content about itself: market reports, whitepapers, thought-leadership posts. Almost none of it is written for the people on the other side of the process. The candidates. The hiring managers who don't do this for a living. The people who just want to know what actually happens to their application after they send it.

We've worked in and around recruitment long enough to know what a recruiter looks for in the first twelve seconds of reading a CV. What a hiring manager notices in an interview that they'll never say to a candidate's face. Why a job advert with fourteen bullet points of requirements gets fewer good applications than one with four. We wrote it down and gave it away.

That's the whole thing. There's no subscription and no premium tier. The only thing we charge for is one-to-one coaching, for anyone who'd like a real person in their corner. The field guides are free because the information is more useful in circulation than behind a paywall.

Free. Without exception. Ever.
Why the name

Why The Com'mon People?

Two reasons, and both are on purpose.

First, it's a nod to Pulp's "Common People". This place is for ordinary working people on both sides of the hiring desk: the candidates, and the managers who hire because it's part of the job, not because it is the job. It isn't the recruitment industry talking to itself.

Second, say the apostrophe out loud. Com'mon. Come on. It's a rallying call. Come on, there's a better way to do this. For the candidate who's been ghosted one too many times, and for the hiring manager wading through fifty applications that all look the same.

So that's what the apostrophe is doing. And yes, it's always spelt with it.

And the logo? Two circles. One is the candidate, one is the person doing the hiring. Where they overlap is where this whole site lives: both sides, understanding each other a bit better.

03 · What we believe

What we believe.

Two applications beat fifty. Always. Spray-and-pray is a numbers game that loses, slowly and painfully, every time. Quality beats volume at every stage of the process.

Job adverts should describe the work. Not the wishlist. Not the culture. Not the perks. The actual work the person will be doing on a Tuesday afternoon.

Candidates deserve to see through a recruiter's eyes. The five-second CV read. The interview tell. The thing the hiring manager noticed but never said. We write it down.

Everything is free. Always. Forever. That's the whole point and it will never change.
